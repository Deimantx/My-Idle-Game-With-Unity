# Purpose

This document defines the complete gameplay loop of the game.

Every gameplay system should support one or more of the gameplay loops described here.

---

# Core Philosophy

The player never directly controls the character.

Instead, the player acts as the strategist.

The player's role is to:

- Choose activities
- Optimize progression
- Unlock new systems
- Manage equipment
- Manage companions
- Decide priorities
- Prepare for harder content

The character performs actions automatically.

The gameplay focuses on decisions, planning, preparation, and progression rather than mechanical execution.

---

# Core Terminology

The game separates progression systems into clear categories.

Professions:

Non-combat progression systems such as Woodcutting, Mining, Fishing, Cooking, Smithing, Carpentry, Herblore, Farming, and other similar systems.

Professions have a maximum level of 100.

Combat Disciplines:

Warrior

Ranger

Mage

Combat Disciplines have a maximum level of 150 and represent long-term combat progression.

Combat Abilities:

Active or auto-used combat actions such as Slash, Shield Block, Fireball, Frost Bolt, Power Shot, and similar abilities.

Companions:

Companions do not use XP or levels.

Companions use ranks from 1–20.

# Short Gameplay Loop (1–5 Minutes)

Typical short session:

- Collect offline rewards
- Claim completed crafting items and XP
- Collect combat loot and XP
- Collect gathered resources and profession XP
- Upgrade equipment
- Spend resources
- Change active profession
- Change active monster
- Start or repeat dungeon
- Manage companions
- Close game

Short sessions should feel useful even if the player only checks the game briefly.

---

# Medium Gameplay Loop (30–60 Minutes)

Typical medium session:

- Train multiple professions
- Craft equipment
- Unlock recipes
- Improve companions
- Fight bosses
- Upgrade profession tools
- Improve combat setup
- Complete achievements
- Unlock new regions
- Prepare for harder content

Medium sessions should allow the player to make meaningful planning decisions.

---

# Long Gameplay Loop (Days / Weeks)

Long-term goals include:

- Max professions
- Level Combat Disciplines
- Complete collection log
- Craft legendary gear
- Unlock every companion
- Complete every dungeon
- Defeat major bosses
- Finish achievements
- Prepare for future updates

The game should always provide long-term goals.

# Professions

Professions are divided into categories.

Current categories:

Gathering

Crafting

Every profession should contribute to another system.

No isolated professions.

Examples:

Woodcutting provides logs for Carpentry.

Mining provides ore for Smithing.

Fishing provides fish for Cooking.

Hunting provides materials for Leatherworking.

Farming and Foraging provide materials for Herblore.

Enchanting improves equipment.

---

# Combat

Combat is real-time and automatic by default.

The player prepares before entering combat.

Preparation matters more than clicking.

The player may use combat abilities manually or set allowed abilities to auto mode.

Enemies also use abilities automatically with cooldowns.

Combat progression is separated into Combat Disciplines:

Warrior

Ranger

Mage

Combat Disciplines are not permanent classes.

The player may train all Combat Disciplines over time.

Hard encounters may require manual ability usage, better preparation, relic timing, food buffs, potions, elixirs, and enemy knowledge.

---

# Crafting

Crafting converts gathered resources into valuable items.

Crafting should never exist independently.

Crafting always supports another gameplay system.

Examples:

Axes improve Woodcutting.

Pickaxes improve Mining.

Equipment improves Combat.

Jewelry improves passive bonuses.

Potions allow healing in combat.

Elixirs provide stronger temporary combat buffs.

Cooking provides reusable food with short-lasting 2–5 minute buffs for combat and professions.

# Risk vs Reward

Harder content should reward better progression.

Hard combat encounters may require manual play instead of full auto mode.

Harder content should reward:

More XP

Better drops

Rare materials

Unique equipment

Achievements

Cosmetics

Progression unlocks

The player should decide whether increased risk is worth it.

# Offline Progress

Offline progression is a core mechanic.

When returning, show a summary.

Display:

Resources gained

XP gained

Items found

Level ups

Rare drops

Time spent

Combat results if offline combat was active

Offline progress should never feel like a punishment.

# Player Decisions

The game should frequently ask questions like:

Should I gather resources?

Should I fight monsters?

Should I craft equipment?

Should I save resources?

Should I sell resources?

Should I unlock a new area?

Should I improve my companion?

Should I train a profession?

Should I train Warrior, Ranger, or Mage?

Should I push harder combat or farm safer content?

Should I craft better tools or better combat gear first?

Good gameplay comes from interesting decisions.

---

# Difficulty Curve

First Hour:

Very simple.

Teach one system at a time.

Early Game:

Introduce professions, inventory, equipment, and basic combat.

Mid Game:

Systems begin interacting.

Players start planning around resources, crafting, equipment, companions, and combat.

Late Game:

Optimization becomes important.

Players prepare for bosses, dungeons, rare materials, and advanced equipment.

End Game:

Multiple progression systems combine together.

Players focus on completion, legendary gear, difficult bosses, collection logs, and long-term account goals.

# Session Design

The game should support:

2 minute sessions

10 minute sessions

30 minute sessions

Several hour sessions

The player should always feel they accomplished something regardless of playtime.

---

# Gameplay Rules

Everything should reward progression.

Every profession should connect to another system.

Every activity should have a purpose.

Combat should connect to professions, equipment, consumables, companions, and progression.

Avoid dead content.

Avoid mandatory daily chores.

Avoid excessive grinding without meaningful rewards.

Always respect player time.

---

# Future Expansion Philosophy

Every future update should expand existing systems rather than replace them.

Examples:

New professions

New regions

New companions

New bosses

New dungeons

New crafting tiers

New equipment

New Combat Disciplines

New combat abilities

New relics

The game should be designed to grow for many years.
